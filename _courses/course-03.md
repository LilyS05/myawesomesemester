## About this course

This semester I'm taking a rhetoric course that traces the Western rhetorical tradition from ancient Athens through today — starting with the Sophists and Plato's disagreements over how speech and persuasion should be taught, and moving all the way up through how rhetoric still shapes law, politics, religion, and culture now. I'm taking it because it's a requirement for my Communication major, but also because understanding how persuasion actually works feels directly useful for the sales side of my career. My hope for the semester is to get better at spotting how arguments are built and where they're weak, so I can both communicate more persuasively myself and see through rhetoric that's being used on me.

## About Professor Buddy 

My professor for this course is Buddy Howell, an advanced instructor in Virginia Tech's School of Communication and one of the university's most decorated teachers — he's received multiple national and university awards for teaching, including the 2024 Excellence in Teaching First-Year Seminars Award from the National Resource Center for the First-Year Experience and Students in Transition, plus student-nominated teaching honors from Omicron Delta Kappa.

### Buddys Research interests

His research focuses on U.S. presidential rhetoric, specifically public diplomacy and campaign/election rhetoric, and his work has appeared in journals like Rhetoric & Public Affairs and the Southern Communication Journal. Given how much of this course is about persuasive rhetoric and its role in politics and society, having someone who studies presidential rhetoric directly — rather than just teaching it in the abstract — feels like a good fit.
