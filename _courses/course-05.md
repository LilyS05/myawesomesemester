## About this course

This semester I'm taking a course on communication and diversity, which examines how media and communication theories intersect with identity — looking at how factors like race, ethnicity, gender, sexual orientation, and ability shape individual and group identity, and how bias, stereotypes, and misrepresentation show up in media, organizations, and everyday interactions. I'm taking it because it's a requirement for my major, and because building real skill in inclusive, culturally aware communication feels essential for sales, where I'm constantly working with a wide range of people, clients, and teams. My hope for the semester is to get better at recognizing bias in messaging (including my own), navigate difficult conversations with more confidence, and communicate in a way that genuinely includes and empowers the people I'm working with.

## About Dr. Uyen Diep 

My professor this semester is Dr. Uyen Diep, an Assistant Professor of Multimedia Journalism at Virginia Tech who earned her PhD from LSU's Manship School of Mass Communication. Before she became an academic, she spent 11 years working as a journalist, including more than five years reporting as a foreign correspondent across Singapore, Malaysia, and the US — so she's someone who's actually lived the international, on-the-ground side of media before turning to study it.

### Research interests

Her research now focuses on digital media, journalism, and political communication, and she's built a strong publishing record with 17 peer-reviewed articles and multiple award-winning conference papers. She's also earned real recognition for her commitment to diversity and inclusion in the field, including a national fellowship for it, plus several prestigious international fellowships tied to journalism and cross-cultural exchange. Between her global reporting background and her focus on inclusive communication, she brings a genuinely lived, well-traveled perspective to a class about diversity in media — not just an academic one.
